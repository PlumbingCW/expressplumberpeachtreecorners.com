Sewer Camera Inspection in Peachtree Corners, GA

A sewer line is invisible until something goes wrong. A camera inspection shows pipe condition, roots, offsets and buildup in minutes, and you can ask for the footage to keep.

It is one of the least costly ways to avoid guessing before you commit to a major repair.

Call (770) 214-4545

What a Camera Inspection Shows

The camera sees what no one can from the surface, and that makes every later decision easier.

A sewer inspection uses a small waterproof camera on a flexible cable, which we feed into the line through a cleanout while we watch a live screen. We note the pipe material, joints, slope and anything unusual, and we record the run.

Typical findings include tree roots at joints, grease buildup, cracked or offset pipe, bellies where water pools, collapsed sections and the exact distance to each problem, and a locator marks that distance on the surface so a repair can be planned without guesswork.

The footage gives you proof that you can share with a county inspector, an insurer or a home buyer. It also lets you compare the line after a repair with the line before, so you can see the work was done.

When to Schedule an Inspection

Several situations make a camera look worthwhile before you spend money on anything else.

  • Repeated backupsIf clearing the line only helps briefly, the cause may be damage or roots.
  • Buying a homeA sewer scope before closing reveals problems the seller may not know about.
  • Before landscaping or a remodelKnow where the line runs and what shape it is in before digging.
  • After a drain cleaningA look afterward confirms the pipe is clean and shows any damage.
  • Aging homesOlder pipe materials may be near the end of their service life.

How Our Inspection Works

Each step is simple, and you see the results on the screen as we go.

  1. Find the access point: We use an existing cleanout or a fixture opening.
  2. Run the camera: We work along the line toward the main and note every defect.
  3. Locate each issue: A locator marks depth and position on the surface.
  4. Review the footage with you: We show you the screen and explain what each finding means.
  5. Give you the recording: You keep the footage for your records.

A camera report gives you facts to compare against Georgia's state minimum plumbing code, which uses the 2024 International Plumbing Code (IPC) as its base. Keep the footage, because it helps with any later repair decision.

Inspections and the Gwinnett County Split

Footage helps most when ownership of the problem is in question.

Gwinnett County Department of Water Resources owns the sewer main and the lateral inside the public right-of-way. The homeowner owns the rest of the lateral. If the camera shows a problem in the county section, the footage supports a request for a county inspection before you pay for repairs. The county asks you to request that inspection if you want to seek reimbursement, and the wait is usually two to three hours.

If the problem sits on your side, the recording tells the plumber exactly what to fix. That avoids paying for exploratory digging and keeps the repair focused on the real failure.

What Changes the Price of an Inspection

Access to the line and the length of the run are the biggest factors in the price.

  • Access pointA cleanout makes the job simple. Without one, we may need to pull a toilet.
  • Line lengthA long run takes more time than a short one.
  • BlockagesA clogged line may need clearing before the camera can pass.
  • Report needsA written summary for a sale or insurer adds time.

You hear the price before the camera goes in, and the recording is yours to keep.
