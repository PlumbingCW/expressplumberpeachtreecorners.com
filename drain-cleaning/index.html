Drain Overflowing Now? Do This While You Wait

A few quick actions limit the mess until the plumber arrives.

  1. Stop using water. Every flush and every load of laundry sends more water toward a drain that cannot take it.
  2. Check whether more than one drain is slow. If a sink, a tub and a toilet all misbehave together, the blockage is probably in the main line.
  3. Skip chemical drain openers. They sit in standing water and can burn skin or damage older pipes. They also make the plumber's job more hazardous.
  4. Move belongings away from the lowest floor drain. Backups surface there first, so lift rugs and clear the area.
  5. Call (770) 214-4545. A dispatcher answers nights, weekends and holidays.

If sewage is reaching the floor, keep children and pets out of the room and wash your hands after any contact.

Signs the Clog Is in Your Main Line

Where the water backs up tells us where the blockage sits.

A single slow fixture usually has a local clog in its trap or branch line. When several fixtures react at once, the problem sits farther downstream. A toilet flush that bubbles in the tub is a classic main-line sign. So is a floor drain that gurgles when the washing machine drains.

Roots, grease, flushed wipes and collapsed sections all cause main-line blockages. Cable machines handle many of them. Stubborn buildup may need hydro jetting, and a repeat blockage calls for a sewer camera inspection to see what the pipe looks like inside.

Who Owns the Blockage? Home, County or Both

Peachtree Corners sewer service is split between you and the county.

Gwinnett County Department of Water Resources owns the sewer main and the part of your lateral that sits inside the public right-of-way. You own the lateral from the edge of the right-of-way to the house. The line between the two is usually near the property edge or the street.

That split matters when a backup stops you from using the house. If the blockage is on your side, you hire a plumber like us. If it might be in the county section, the county can inspect before you pay for repairs, and the county's own policy on reimbursement is worth asking about. Expect a wait of two to three hours for that inspection, so call early.

We locate the blockage by camera and tell you which section it sits in before you decide who to call next.

How We Clear a Drain, Step by Step

We start light and escalate only when the pipe demands it.

  1. Confirm the scope. We run water, check the cleanout and note which fixtures react.
  2. Cable the line. A cable machine with the right cutter head opens the clog for most sinks, tubs and branch lines.
  3. Look with a camera when the cause is unclear. The camera shows roots, grease, scale or a broken joint.
  4. Jet if the buildup is heavy. High-pressure water scours the pipe wall instead of just poking a hole through the blockage.
  5. Test the flow. We run water until the drain moves freely and explain what we found.

Traps, vents and cleanouts all fall under Georgia's state minimum plumbing code, which is built on the 2024 International Plumbing Code (IPC). After a cleaning we leave each of them in working order rather than patching around them.

What Changes the Price of Drain Cleaning

The right price depends on the pipe, not on a flat rate.

  • Where the clog sitsA trap or a short branch line takes less time than a main line that runs under the yard.
  • AccessA reachable cleanout makes the job fast. No cleanout may mean pulling a toilet to get a cable in.
  • Method neededCabling is the simplest method. Camera work and hydro jetting add equipment and time.
  • Hour of the callEmergency calls at night or on weekends include after-hours labor.

You hear the price before we start, and we stop to talk if the cause turns out to be larger than expected.

Keep Drains Clear After We Leave

A few habits prevent most repeat clogs.

Pour cooking grease into a can and put it in the trash. Grease cools in the pipe and builds a plug that cable machines struggle to clear. Use a strainer in the shower and kitchen sink to catch hair and food.

Flush only toilet paper. Wipes labeled flushable do not break down like paper and they cause many of the main-line calls we see. If a mature tree sits near the sewer line, schedule a camera look every year or two, because roots enter through tiny joints and grow quietly. When roots have already damaged the pipe, sewer line repair stops the cycle that cleaning alone cannot.
