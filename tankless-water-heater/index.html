Tankless Water Heater Installation in Peachtree Corners, GA

A tankless heater warms water only when you ask for it, and it frees the floor space a tank used to hold. It also asks more of your gas line, venting and installer than a tank does.

We check all three before we recommend one, so the unit you buy fits the house.

Is Tankless Right for Your House?

It suits some homes very well and others only with changes.

A tankless heater fires a burner or an element only while hot water is flowing, which removes standby heat loss and the bulky tank. The trade-off is that the heater must raise the temperature of water quickly, which takes serious gas flow or electric capacity.

What Has to Change in the House

Plan for four site items before ordering a unit.

  • Gas supplyTankless units draw a lot of gas at once. We check pipe size and meter capacity.
  • VentingMost units need sealed venting that differs from a tank flue.
  • ElectricalEven gas units need a power outlet for the controls and fan.
  • Condensate drainHigh-efficiency units produce acidic condensate that needs a safe drain path.

Gas Safety Comes First

Know what to do if something smells wrong.

If you ever smell gas near a heater, leave the building immediately. Call Atlanta Gas Light at 877.427.4321 or 911 from outside before you call a plumber. Do not use switches or a phone inside.

After any gas work we test every joint for leaks and check that the exhaust reaches the outdoors, because a tankless unit that is vented wrong can send combustion gases into living space.

Sizing by How Many Fixtures Run at Once

Capacity is about flow, not tank volume.

A tankless unit is rated by how much water it can heat per minute at a given temperature rise, so a shower, a dishwasher and a washing machine running together can exceed a small unit. A large family needs a bigger heater or two units.

We count the fixtures in your house, ask which run at the same time and then recommend a capacity, and that conversation matters more than the brand on the box. An undersized tankless heater is the most common regret we hear about.

Cold winter groundwater lowers the incoming temperature, so a unit that works well in summer may feel weaker in January, which is why we size for the coldest realistic day.

Gas and venting work on a tankless unit falls under the state minimum codes, the 2024 International Plumbing Code (IPC) and the 2024 International Fuel Gas Code, both in effect since January 1, 2025. A licensed plumber should size the gas line and vent for the model you choose.

Tankless Maintenance That Keeps It Working

Scale is the main enemy of an on-demand heater.

Mineral scale builds inside the heat exchanger and slows flow over time. A yearly flush with a descaling solution removes it. The right interval depends on your water. Test your tap or ask Gwinnett County Department of Water Resources for current water quality information, because we do not assume hardness for any address.

Keep the intake and exhaust clear, and clean the inlet screen when the manufacturer calls for it. If error codes appear or the water turns lukewarm, call early, because small sensor problems are cheaper than a damaged exchanger.
