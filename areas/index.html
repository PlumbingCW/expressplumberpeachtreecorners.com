Why These Four Towns

Each one connects to our office by a short, direct route.

Norcross borders Peachtree Corners on the south. Duluth lies to the east along Peachtree Industrial Boulevard and I-85. Dunwoody touches us on the west, at Winters Chapel Road. Johns Creek sits across the Chattahoochee River to the north, reached through the GA 141 corridor.

Each page lists the ZIP codes we serve there, the utility that handles water and sewer, and what to prepare before a crew arrives. The details differ because the towns differ. Dunwoody is in DeKalb County, Johns Creek is in Fulton County, and Norcross and Duluth are in Gwinnett County.

Nearby Places We Also Reach

Some addresses fall just outside the four main pages.

Doraville and Berkeley Lake lie close to our office, and crews reach them regularly. Chamblee and Sandy Springs sit west of Dunwoody, and Suwanee lies northeast of Duluth. We mention these places so you know a call is worth making even if your town does not have a page.

Dispatch decides coverage by street address, not by town name. Give the dispatcher your address and the nearest cross street, and we will confirm whether a crew can reach you and how soon.

Utilities Change at the County Line

The right phone number for a street-side problem depends on where you live.

Gwinnett County Department of Water Resources serves Peachtree Corners, Norcross and Duluth. DeKalb County Watershed Management serves Dunwoody. The North Fulton system serves Johns Creek.

Each utility handles public mains and meters. Everything on your side of the meter is a private repair, and that is where we work. Our town pages list the emergency numbers for each utility so you can call the right one first when the whole street is affected.

A quick rule helps. If neighbors on your block lost water or see sewage in the street, the problem is probably public and the utility should hear about it first. If only your house is affected, the problem is probably private, and a plumber is the right call.
