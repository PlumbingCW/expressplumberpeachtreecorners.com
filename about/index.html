What We Do

Emergency repairs and planned plumbing work for homes and small businesses.

We repair leaks, clear drains, replace water heaters, fix sewer lines and handle the work that follows. Our services page lists everything we do. Most calls begin as emergencies, so our crews carry the tools to stop damage first and finish the repair second.

We serve Peachtree Corners, Norcross, Duluth, Dunwoody and Johns Creek. Our areas page explains each town and the utilities that serve it.

Commercial customers are welcome as well. Restaurants, offices and retail suites call us for repairs that need to happen outside business hours, and our commercial plumbing page explains how that work goes.

How We Price

You hear the number before the work.

Every job is priced from the conditions on site. The plumber explains the scope, tells you the price and waits for your approval. If we find something that changes the job, we stop and talk to you before continuing.

We do not quote prices over the phone for work we have not seen, because honest numbers need real conditions. We can explain the factors that will matter, such as access, parts and the hour of the call.

What Happens on a Typical Call

The same few questions start every job.

  1. Dispatch asks what is happening. We need to know what is leaking, where the shutoff is and whether anyone is at risk.
  2. We explain the likely cause. Many problems have a short list of causes, and knowing them helps you decide whether to wait for a crew or act now.
  3. A plumber arrives and checks the system. The plumber looks at the fixture, the pipe and the shutoff before suggesting a repair.
  4. You approve the work. Nothing starts until you have heard the scope and the price.
  5. We test the repair. Pressure tests and flow tests confirm the problem is solved before the crew leaves.

We keep notes on what we find so that a repeat visit does not start from zero. If a problem points to a larger issue, such as an aging supply line or a failing sewer lateral, we explain the options and leave the decision with you.

What Peachtree Corners Homes Need From a Plumber

The housing mix shapes the work.

The city's 2018 long-range plan counts about 59 percent of housing units as single-family and about one in five units in buildings of ten or more. Single-family owners handle their own water heater, meter box and sewer lateral. Multifamily residents rely on property managers, and managers need a plumber who can reach several units quickly.

Because many neighborhoods date to the 1970s, we spend a lot of time on aging drain lines, original supply piping and water heaters that have outlived their tanks. Gwinnett County Department of Water Resources serves the city with water and sewer, and the county's responsibility ends at the meter and the edge of the right-of-way. Anything past those points is the owner's, which is why a clear explanation of ownership belongs in every repair conversation.

Licensing and Insurance

You should be able to check us.

Georgia requires a licensed master plumber to operate a plumbing business. The state publishes a free lookup called GOALS, run through the Secretary of State, where you can search any licensee. The Georgia State Division of Master and Journeyman Plumbers describes the license classes. A Class I master plumber may work on single-family and one-level dwellings up to two families, and on commercial buildings under 10,000 square feet. A Class II license has no such limits.

Ask any plumber, including us, which class applies to your job. We are licensed and insured, and we welcome the question.

Why the Office Sits on Holcomb Bridge Road

Location is part of how we serve you.

Holcomb Bridge Road runs through the heart of Peachtree Corners, close to GA 141 and Peachtree Parkway. From the office, crews reach Norcross, Duluth and Dunwoody directly. Johns Creek is reached across the Chattahoochee on the GA 141 corridor.

That position shortens the drive on most calls, and it keeps our response times predictable for the neighborhoods we know best. Dispatch still gives you a realistic window rather than a promise, because traffic and crew availability change by the hour. We would rather tell you the truth than hurry you into a wrong expectation.

If your address sits just outside the towns we list, call anyway. Dispatch will tell you whether a crew can reach you and how long the trip is likely to take, so you can decide with real information.
