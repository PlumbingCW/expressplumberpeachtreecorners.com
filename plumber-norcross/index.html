Burst Pipe or Backup in Norcross? Do This First

Fast action limits damage while the truck is on its way.

  1. Close the main shutoff valve right away. Look where the water line enters the house, or at the meter box in the front yard. Turn the handle clockwise until it stops.
  2. Turn off the water heater. Switch off its breaker, or set a gas unit to pilot, so the tank does not run dry and overheat.
  3. Cut power in wet rooms. Use the breaker panel from a dry spot and stay out of any room where outlets sit in water.
  4. Catch what you can. Buckets and towels buy time. Photograph the damage before you clean up.
  5. Call (770) 214-4545. Dispatch answers around the clock, including holidays.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 from outside before you call us.

Norcross Plumbing Across Old Streets and Newer Utilities

History explains some of the leaks, and the utility map explains who to call.

Norcross was chartered on October 26, 1870, and its historic district covers about 112 acres around Thrasher Park. Streets such as Pitch Tree Road hold restored Victorian homes. Houses of that age can carry a mix of original and replaced pipe, so a leak may sit behind a patched wall rather than behind fresh material. We trace the source with leak detection equipment before anyone cuts into plaster.

Norcross runs its own public power, but Gwinnett County took over the city's water and sewer system on May 13, 2013. That means a street-side water or sewer problem goes to Gwinnett County Department of Water Resources, which runs a 24-hour dispatch line at 678-376-7000. If the whole block lost water, call the county first. If only your house is affected, call us.

I-85 marks the southern edge of the city, and Norcross shares its northern border with Peachtree Corners. Traffic on I-85 and Peachtree Industrial can slow any truck, so we ask for cross streets and a nearby landmark during the call.

Georgia's state minimum plumbing code, based on the 2024 International Plumbing Code (IPC), applies in Norcross as it does everywhere in the state. Permit handling is local, so ask the office that issues permits for your address before a larger project begins.
