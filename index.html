Plumbing Emergency Right Now? Start Here

These five steps limit damage while a plumber is on the way.

  1. Shut off the main water valve. It is usually where the line enters the house or in the meter box in the front yard. Turn it clockwise until it stops.
  2. Keep power away from standing water. If outlets or appliances sit in water, switch off the breaker from a dry spot and stay out of the room.
  3. Open the lowest faucet. Draining the lines relieves pressure and slows the leak.
  4. Photograph the damage and move valuables. Pictures help with an insurance claim later.
  5. Call (770) 214-4545. Dispatch is open nights, weekends and holidays.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 from outside before you call a plumber. Gas problems are never a wait-and-see situation.

How We Price Plumbing Work

You get the number before the work, not after.

Every job is priced from the actual conditions, so the figure you hear is for your home, not an average. These factors move the price the most:

  • Scope of the repairA cartridge swap and a sewer replacement are different jobs, and the quote reflects that.
  • Access to the pipeOpen basements and utility closets are quick. Pipes under slabs or behind finished walls take longer.
  • Parts and materialsFixture grade, pipe material and code-required fittings change the parts bill.
  • Time of dayCalls that need a crew at night, on weekends or on holidays carry after-hours labor.

We price in writing or out loud before work starts, and we stop to talk to you if we find something that changes the scope.

What Plumbing in Peachtree Corners Looks Like

The city's housing and utilities shape the calls we take.

Peachtree Corners incorporated in 2012 and sits in the southwest corner of Gwinnett County. The city's 2018 long-range plan counts about 59 percent of its housing as single-family. That means most calls involve a house with its own water heater, meter box and sewer lateral, and each of those parts has an owner who has to maintain it.

Water and sewer service comes from the Gwinnett County Department of Water Resources. The county meter sits in the front yard under a metal cover. Pipes and fixtures on the house side of that meter belong to the homeowner, so a leak between the meter and the foundation is a private repair. Our leak detection work locates those breaks before they turn into a flooded yard or a surprise bill.

About one in five homes in the city sits in a building with ten or more units. Shared risers and common drain stacks mean one unit's backup can reach several others. Property managers should call us the moment a second unit reports a slow drain.

Technology Park began in the late 1960s, and neighborhoods such as Peachtree Station and Spalding Corners followed in the 1970s. Houses from that era are now over fifty years old, so original drain lines, supply lines and fixtures deserve a professional look before they fail at an inconvenient hour.

Licensed, Insured and Easy to Verify

Trust should not depend on our word alone.

Georgia requires plumbing contractors to be licensed, and the Secretary of State publishes a free licensee search called GOALS. Ask any plumber for a license number and look it up before work begins. We expect that question and welcome it.

Our crews carry insurance for the work they do inside your home. If a repair needs a permit, we tell you at the quote stage, and we direct you to the Peachtree Corners Building Division for the local requirements. Permit rules vary by job, so we never guess at them on the phone.

Georgia's state minimum plumbing code is based on the 2024 International Plumbing Code (IPC) and took effect on January 1, 2025. Any plumber you hire should work to that standard, and you can ask us how it applies to your job.
