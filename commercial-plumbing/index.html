Business Plumbing Emergency? Protect the Space First

Fast action keeps a repair from becoming a closure.

  1. Close the nearest shutoff. Know where the main and the branch valves are before you ever need them. Turn the valve to the closed position and confirm the flow has stopped.
  2. Cut power to wet equipment. Switch off breakers for anything sitting in or near water, including coolers, ice machines and registers.
  3. Move stock and records off the floor. Even a thin film of water wicks into cardboard and drywall within minutes.
  4. Keep customers and staff out of the wet zone. Wet tile and shared restrooms are slip hazards, and sewage needs a closed door.
  5. Photograph everything and call (770) 214-4545. Pictures support your insurance claim, and dispatch answers nights, weekends and holidays.

If you smell gas in a commercial space, evacuate everyone and call the gas utility or 911 from outside.

Backflow, Grease Traps and Storm Drains

Three systems quietly protect your building and the public lines.

Backflow preventers keep dirty water from running backward into the clean supply. Many commercial properties have them, and testing questions go to the county's backflow program at 678.376.4213. We tell you what we see on site and point you to the county for what it requires.

Grease traps and interceptors catch fats before they reach the sewer. A full trap sends grease into the lateral, where it hardens and blocks the line. Restaurant owners should ask us about cleaning frequency after the first blockage rather than after the third.

Storm drains carry rain away from lots and loading areas. Debris clogs them quietly, and the first sign is standing water at a door. We clear what is on your property and flag anything that belongs to the county.

Apartments and Shared Risers in Peachtree Corners

The city's building mix puts shared plumbing in a large share of homes.

The city's 2018 long-range plan counts about 20 percent of homes in buildings with ten or more units and 21 percent in buildings with two to nine units. That is a lot of households on shared stacks and risers. One blocked stack can back up several units, so a manager who hears from two residents should call before a third reports water.

Owners and renters each make up a large share of households, which affects who calls us. Tenants usually report the problem and the owner or manager approves the repair. We explain the cause in writing-friendly language so the person who pays can decide quickly.

Water and sewer come from the Gwinnett County Department of Water Resources, and the county's responsibility stops at the meter. Everything on the building side belongs to the property owner.

How We Keep Your Business Open

We plan the job around your trading hours.
