No Hot Water? Quick Checks Before You Call

General guidance for tank water heaters.

  1. Check the breaker or pilot. An electric unit has its own breaker. A gas unit has a pilot or igniter. Reset a tripped breaker once, not repeatedly.
  2. Look at the base of the tank. Water pooling there means a leak. Close the cold supply valve above the tank and turn off power or gas.
  3. Feel the hot pipes. If hot water is weak at one faucet only, the fixture may be the problem, not the heater.
  4. Do not remove panels or relief valves. Leave those to a plumber.
  5. Gas smell? Leave the house and call 911 from outside.

Questions to Ask Before You Repair or Replace

General checklist for any tank water heater decision.

  • How old is the unit? The date is on the rating plate, and the serial number often encodes it.
  • Where is it leaking? A drip at a fitting is a different job from a leak through the tank wall.
  • What fuel does it use? Gas and electric units need different parts, venting and permits.
  • What does the plumber propose, and why? Ask for the reasoning and the cost of each option before work begins.
  • What happens to the old tank? Ask who hauls it away.

None of this depends on where you live. It is simply the list worth having in hand when the plumber arrives, so the visit starts with your questions answered. The replacement page explains the usual steps in order.
