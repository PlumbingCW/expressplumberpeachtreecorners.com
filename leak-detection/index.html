Run These Two Quick Checks First

Gwinnett County gives homeowners two simple tests to confirm a leak.

The water meter sits in the front yard under a metal cover. Gwinnett County Department of Water Resources suggests a two-hour test. Turn off every faucet and appliance that uses water, record the meter reading, wait two hours and read it again. If the leak-indicator wheel moves or the reading changes, water is escaping somewhere.

For toilets, add a few drops of dye to the tank, wait 30 minutes without flushing and look in the bowl. Color in the bowl means the flapper is leaking. The county notes that a running toilet can waste more than 200 gallons a day, and that leaks overall can waste more than 10,000 gallons a year.

Keep the meter box clear of objects within one foot so the meter can be read and reached.

Water Pouring Through a Ceiling? Do This Now

Active leaks need fast action.

  1. Shut off the main water valve: It is near where the line enters the house or in the meter box.
  2. Turn off power in wet areas: Use the breaker if outlets or fixtures are in the water path.
  3. Catch and redirect water: Buckets and towels protect floors and belongings.
  4. Photograph everything: Pictures support an insurance claim.
  5. Call (770) 214-4545: Dispatch answers nights, weekends and holidays.

How We Find a Leak You Cannot See

Several tools work together, so the opening is small.

  1. Pressure test the system: A drop in pressure with fixtures off confirms a leak and shows how large it is.
  2. Listen with acoustic gear: Sensitive microphones pick up the sound of water escaping from pressurized pipe.
  3. Scan with thermal imaging: Temperature patterns show wet material behind walls and under floors.
  4. Inspect by camera where it helps: A small camera can see inside drain lines and through small openings.
  5. Mark the spot: We tell you exactly where to open, so the repair is a patch and not a demolition.

Signs of a Hidden Leak

Your house usually tells you before the bill does.

A water bill that rises with no change in habits is the most common sign. Others include the sound of running water when every tap is off, damp or warm spots on a floor, a musty smell and a meter that moves with everything shut.

The EPA says the average household wastes more than 9,300 gallons a year to leaks, and that about 9 percent of homes leak 50 gallons or more a day. Small drips matter because they run all day. A faucet dripping once a second can waste over 3,000 gallons a year.

Once the leak is located, any repair to the piping falls under Georgia's state minimum plumbing code, which uses the 2024 International Plumbing Code (IPC) as its base. The detection report helps you decide on the repair with clear facts in hand.
