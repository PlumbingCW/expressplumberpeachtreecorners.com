Slow Drain or Backup? What to Try Before You Call

General guidance for any house.

  1. Find out how many drains are affected. One slow sink points to that fixture's line. Several fixtures at once point to the main line.
  2. Stop running water. Hold off on laundry, dishwashers and showers while a backup is active.
  3. Locate the cleanout. It is usually a capped pipe near the foundation or in the yard. Clear mulch and leaves from around it.
  4. Skip chemical drain products. They can splash on a plumber and do little against a blockage in the main line.
  5. Call (770) 214-4545 and tell dispatch which fixtures are affected.

What a Sewer Camera Shows

General information, not a finding about any house here.

A sewer camera is a small waterproof lens on a flexible cable. The plumber feeds it into the line through the cleanout and watches a live image on a screen. It can show a root mass, a sagging section where water pools, a cracked joint or a clean pipe that simply needs the blockage cleared.

The footage lets you choose between clearing the line, repairing one section or leaving it alone, with the evidence in front of you. If you want a record, ask the plumber to save the video. Our sewer camera inspection page describes the process in more detail, and a camera run is often the first step before any sewer repair is priced.
