Main Line Backing Up? Do This Before the Jetter Arrives

These steps protect your floors and belongings while we travel to your address.

  1. Stop running water: Do not use sinks, showers or the washing machine until the line is open again.
  2. Do not flush again: Another flush only adds to the overflow and makes the cleanup larger.
  3. Locate your cleanout: It is a capped pipe near the house or the property line, and you should not open it if water is rising.
  4. Protect the lowest floor: Lift rugs and move belongings away from floor drains so they stay dry.

Jetting Compared With Cabling

The two methods solve different problems, and choosing between them depends on the pipe and the buildup.

A cable machine spins a blade through a clog and pulls it apart. It is quick and works well for simple blockages, but it can leave a coating of grease and scale behind, which is why the same drain slows again a few months later.

A jetting machine sends water at high pressure through a nozzle, and the water scours the pipe wall for its full length, flushes debris downstream and cuts light roots.

What Hydro Jetting Clears

Four common buildups respond well to a water jet when the pipe is sound.

Grease

Cooking fat hardens in the line and narrows it. Jetting cuts it loose and flushes it out.

Mineral scale

Scale builds on pipe walls over years. High-pressure water breaks it up.

Light roots

Fine roots in joints can be cut back. Heavy root damage often needs repair.

Sludge and debris

Settled waste is flushed downstream instead of just pushed aside.

Our Jetting Process

We always look inside the pipe before we blast anything through it.

  1. Camera the line: We see the pipe condition, the pipe material and the exact blockage before anything else happens.
  2. Decide if the pipe can take it: Cracked, collapsed or badly corroded pipe may need repair before it can be jetted.
  3. Connect to the cleanout: The nozzle goes in through the cleanout or a fixture access point near the main.
  4. Jet in passes: We work in stages so debris flows out in controlled amounts instead of all at once.
  5. Camera again: A second look confirms the pipe is clear and shows any damage the buildup was hiding.

When Jetting Is the Wrong Tool

Water pressure can hurt weak pipe, so we choose the method with care.

Very old cast iron, brittle clay and sections that have already cracked can break under pressure, and a collapsed line cannot be jetted open at all. In those cases a camera inspection and a sewer line repair are the honest answer.

We tell you what the camera shows before we choose a method. If jetting is unsafe for your pipe, we say so plainly and explain the alternatives that fit your situation.

Kitchens, Multifamily Buildings and Heavy Use

Buildings with shared lines see more grease and more repeat backups.

About one in five homes in Peachtree Corners sits in a building with ten or more units, according to the city's 2018 long-range plan, and shared drain stacks collect grease and wipes from many households. One backup can reach several units, so property managers often schedule jetting on a regular cycle to prevent emergencies.

Restaurants and food service tenants face the same risk from kitchen grease, and our commercial plumbing crews work around business hours to limit downtime.
