Sewage Backing Up? Protect the House First

Keep contact with sewage to a minimum and limit the spread.

  1. Stop all water use: No flushing, no showers and no laundry until the line has been cleared and checked.
  2. Keep people and pets away from affected floors: Sewage carries bacteria that can make people sick if they touch it.
  3. Do not use chemical drain cleaners: They rarely help in a sewer line, and they create a hazard for the crew.
  4. Photograph the damage: You may need the pictures for an insurance claim or for a request to the county.
  5. Call (770) 214-4545: Dispatch answers nights, weekends and holidays.

If sewage has soaked carpet or drywall, wear gloves and wash up afterward. Porous materials often need professional removal.

Who Owns Which Part of the Sewer Line

Peachtree Corners follows a split set by Gwinnett County.

Gwinnett County Department of Water Resources owns the sewer main and the part of your lateral inside the public right-of-way. The homeowner owns the lateral from the edge of the right-of-way to the house. Roots or debris from the county section can create liability for the county, and the county asks you to request a Department of Water Resources inspection before you make repairs if you want to seek reimbursement. Expect a two to three hour wait for that inspection.

We camera the whole run and tell you where the break sits. If it falls in the county section, we give you the footage and the location to share. If it falls on your side, we quote the repair.

Signs a Sewer Line Is Damaged

The house gives clear hints before a full failure.

Repeated backups

Clearing the line works for a short time and then the problem returns.

Gurgling and slow drains

Several fixtures slow together, and toilets bubble when other drains run.

Sewage smell

An odor in the yard or near a drain can mean a broken pipe or a dry trap.

Soft or green patches

Wet ground or unusually green grass above the line points to a leak.

Foundation or slab signs

Settling or cracks near the pipe route can accompany a broken line.

A sewer camera inspection confirms these signs and records the pipe condition.

We lay out the options with the reasons for each. Heavy root problems often justify repair so that sewer line cleaning does not become a recurring bill.

Sewer repairs should follow Georgia's state minimum plumbing code, which is built on the 2024 International Plumbing Code (IPC). Ask the Building Division about permits and about county inspection of the section inside the right-of-way.

Our Repair Process

From the first camera pass to the final flow test.

  1. Locate the break: A camera run and a locator mark the damage on the surface so the dig stays as small as it can.
  2. Present the options: You see the footage, the repair choices and the price before any digging starts.

Trees, Roots and the Peachtree Corners Lot

Mature trees are common in the area, and so are the root problems they cause.

Peachtree Corners covers about 17 square miles, and parks such as the 227-acre Simpsonwood Park sit beside tree-lined neighborhoods. Roots seek moisture and enter sewer joints through tiny gaps, so a pipe that looks fine today can be half full of roots in a few seasons.

Before you plant near the line, call 811 to mark utilities, because Georgia 811 is free. If a tree is already near the line, a camera look every year or two catches roots early, when the fix is still small. Our Duluth service team sees the same root patterns in the older neighborhoods east of the city.
