Disposal Jammed or Leaking? Safe Steps First

Never put a hand into a disposal, even when it is off.

  1. Turn off the switch and unplug the unit. If it is hardwired, switch off the breaker. A disposal that restarts unexpectedly causes serious injuries.
  2. Stop running water in the sink. A leaking unit sends water into the cabinet with every use.
  3. Look for the source of the leak. A wet top flange, side hose or bottom seam each point to a different repair.
  4. Empty the cabinet and set a bowl under the unit.
  5. Call (770) 214-4545. Dispatch answers nights, weekends and holidays.

If the sink is full of standing water, do not pour chemical cleaner in. It splashes back when a plumber starts work.

Repair or Replace the Disposal

A unit that leaks from the housing cannot be saved.

Jams, tripped resets and loose hoses are quick repairs. A cracked housing is not repairable, and a motor that burns out repeatedly is a signal to replace. Replacement units come in different horsepower ratings, and a bigger motor handles tougher scraps with less strain.

We install the new unit, reconnect the dishwasher drain and test the sink for leaks. If the old unit leaves pitting or corrosion on the flange, we mention it before you buy a new one.

Many disposal issues start with what goes down the drain, because fibrous peels, grease and coffee grounds clump in the trap below the unit and make a healthy motor look like a bad one. That is why we always test the drain beyond the disposal before we recommend spending money on a new unit, since a replacement installed over a clogged trap will fail the same way within weeks.

How We Handle a Disposal Call

The plumber works from the top of the sink to the sewer line.

  1. We make it safe. Power is cut and the unit is checked before any hand goes near it.
  2. We find the leak or jam. Water and a flashlight show where the leak starts, and the hex key frees most jams.
  3. We repair or swap. Seals, gaskets and hoses are replaced, or the unit comes out.
  4. We check the trap and drain line. Clogs below the disposal are cleared so the repair holds.
  5. We run water and test. The sink fills and drains while we watch every joint.

Kitchens in Peachtree Corners Homes

Most local calls involve a house kitchen with its own drain line.

About 59 percent of Peachtree Corners housing is single-family, according to the city's 2018 long-range plan. Single-family kitchens usually drain through a short branch into the main stack. A clog that starts at the disposal can slow the whole branch if it is not cleared.

If every sink in the house is slow at once, the problem is farther down the line. In that case our sewer line cleaning crews clear the main, and the disposal gets a clean bill of health.

Rental kitchens in the city's larger buildings often share drain stacks, so a tenant who reports a slow kitchen sink may be seeing a stack problem that affects other units. Property managers should call us early, because a stack clog that starts in one kitchen can surface in a unit two floors below.

Habits That Keep a Disposal Alive

Small changes save the motor and the drain.

Run cold water before, during and after each use. Cold water keeps grease solid so it moves through the unit. Cut large scraps down before feeding them in. Skip bones, fibrous vegetables, pasta, rice and anything starchy that swells.

Never pour grease in the sink, with or without a disposal. Grease cools and coats the pipe wall. If a disposal keeps clogging after repair, a camera check shows whether the drain beyond it has damage.
