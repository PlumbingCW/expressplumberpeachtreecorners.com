Sink Not Draining but Not Clogged in Peachtree Corners, GA

A sink that holds water even though the trap is clean usually has a partial blockage further down, a blocked vent, or a problem shared with other fixtures. The quick checks below sort out which one it is.

Most of them take only a few minutes and no special tools.

Why a Sink Drains Slowly Without a Visible Clog

A drain needs three things to work well. It needs a clear pipe, a downward slope and air behind the water. When any one is missing, water sits in the basin even though the visible parts look fine.

A partial clog is the most common reason. Soap, hair and grease coat the pipe walls and narrow the opening. Water still gets through, only slowly. The trap under the sink may be clean while the pipe beyond it is half full of residue.

The second cause is a vent problem. Plumbing vents let air in behind draining water. If a vent is blocked by a bird nest, leaves or ice, the drain gurgles and slows. The third cause is a shared line that is blocked further down, which affects several fixtures at once.

Quick Checks Before You Call

Start with the simplest tests and work outward. Each one rules out a cause.

  1. Run the other fixtures. If the tub or a nearby sink is also slow, the problem is farther down the line.
  2. Listen for gurgling. Bubbling sounds in a toilet or tub when the sink drains point to an air or venting problem.
  3. Remove the stopper or strainer. Pull out hair and residue by hand.
  4. Open the trap. Place a bucket under it and unscrew the slip nuts to check for buildup.
  5. Pour in hot tap water. If it drains for a moment and then slows again, grease is a likely culprit.

If any of these produce a clear result, you have your answer. If none do, the cause is beyond the trap.

The Disposal and Dishwasher Connection

Kitchen sinks have extra ways to go wrong. A garbage disposal that is partly jammed leaves ground food in its chamber, and the water backs up even when the sink pipe is open. Make sure the disposal is off, then check for a stuck flywheel using the wrench slot underneath.

The dishwasher drain hose often ties into the disposal or the sink tailpiece. If that connection is clogged, dishwasher water can back into the sink or the sink can drain slowly while the dishwasher runs. Look for a high loop in the hose and clear the air gap if your sink has one.

If the disposal hums but does not spin, or leaks at the seams, our garbage disposal repair service handles both repair and replacement.

Shared Stacks in Peachtree Corners Buildings

About one in five homes in Peachtree Corners sits in a building with ten or more units, according to the city's 2018 plan. In those buildings, several apartments share drain stacks and vents.

A slow sink in one unit can reflect a problem in the shared stack. Tell your property manager when neighbors report the same symptom, because a shared problem is not fixed by clearing one trap. Single-family owners have a simpler case. The drain line leaves the house on your property, and you own the part that runs beyond the public right-of-way.

Signs the Problem Is in the Branch or Main Line

Water that drains slowly from every fixture on one side of the house points to a branch line problem. Water that drains slowly everywhere points to the main line. A floor drain that gurgles when the washing machine drains is another sign.

Why Chemical Openers Make Things Worse

Liquid drain openers are tempting, but they rarely solve a partial blockage. They sit in the pipe and may eat through soft residue without clearing the whole run. If a plumber then opens the trap, the chemicals can splash onto skin or eyes.

Chemicals can also damage older metal pipes and soften some plastic parts. If they fail to work, the standing water in your sink is now hazardous. A plunger, a hand auger or a cable machine is safer and more effective.

If you already used a chemical product, tell the plumber when they arrive. They will wear the right protection and work with more care.

Vent Problems Explained in Plain Terms

Every drain pipe in your house connects to a vent pipe that rises through the roof. The vent lets fresh air enter the system so that water can flow smoothly. Without air, water behaves like liquid in a bottle turned upside down. It glugs and slows instead of flowing freely.

A blocked vent shows up in a few typical ways. You may hear gurgling from a nearby drain or toilet when water runs. The sink may drain well for a moment and then stall. Sometimes you smell sewer gas near a fixture because the water in the trap gets pulled out.

You can sometimes see a vent problem from the yard. Look at the vent pipes on the roof for leaves, nests or debris. Do not climb onto a roof without proper safety gear. A plumber can clear a vent with a cable or a hose and can tell whether the vent route itself is faulty. If a remodel moved a sink without adding a proper vent, the drain may never have worked well in the first place.

Preventing the Next Slow Drain

Wipe greasy pans with a paper towel before washing them. Use a mesh strainer in the kitchen sink and a hair catcher in the shower. Run hot water for about half a minute after washing dishes to move residue along.

Flush a drain monthly with a mild solution of hot water and dish soap if you are prone to buildup. Avoid putting coffee grounds, eggshells or fibrous vegetables in the disposal. These materials pack into pipes and form dense plugs.

If a slowdown returns within weeks of being cleared, ask for a camera inspection. A rough pipe wall, a low spot or a root intrusion will keep catching debris until it is repaired.
