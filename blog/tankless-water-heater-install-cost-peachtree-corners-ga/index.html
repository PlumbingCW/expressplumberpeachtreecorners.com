Tankless Water Heater Install Cost in Peachtree Corners, GA

Tankless installation cost depends mostly on the fuel, the venting route, the gas line size and how much of your existing plumbing must change. Two houses on the same street can receive very different quotes for the same unit.

This guide explains each cost driver so you can read a quote line by line.

What Sets the Price of a Tankless Installation

The unit itself is only one line on the bill. Labor, venting, gas piping, electrical work and disposal of the old tank all change the total. A simple swap in a garage with a gas line already nearby sits at one end. A conversion from an electric tank in a closet to a gas tankless unit on an exterior wall sits at the other.

That is why a phone price without a visit is a rough guess at best. A plumber needs to see the current heater, the gas meter, the path to an outside wall and the shutoff valves before pricing the work with confidence.

Gas or Electric: Why Fuel Changes the Job

Gas tankless units use a powerful burner and exhaust combustion gases outside. That means a vent pipe, a combustion air plan and a gas line that can feed the burner at full fire. Electric units skip the vent but draw a very large electrical load. Many panels need an upgrade to support them, and that work is done by a licensed electrician.

If you already have a gas tank heater, staying with gas often keeps the project simpler. If you have an electric tank, moving to gas adds a gas line and a vent. Moving to an electric tankless unit adds a circuit and possibly a panel change.

Heat pump water heaters are a different path. The Department of Energy says they can be two to three times as efficient as standard electric resistance models. They also need about 1,000 cubic feet of air space and a location that stays between 40 and 90 degrees Fahrenheit. A small utility closet usually does not qualify.

Peachtree Corners Water Notes for Tankless Buyers

Gwinnett County Department of Water Resources serves Peachtree Corners, drawing from Lake Lanier through the Shoal Creek and Lanier filter plants. The utility's annual report does not give a hardness figure, so test your own tap with a simple kit or ask the utility directly before assuming anything about scale.

That matters for a tankless unit, because mineral buildup shortens the time between flushes. The county also does not control the temperature of water inside your house. Hot water trouble is a plumbing job, which is why a Norcross plumber or Duluth plumber is the right call rather than the utility.

Source: Gwinnett County Department of Water Resources, 2025 water quality report and customer guidance.

Venting and Gas Sizing Are Safety Work

A tankless gas heater puts out carbon monoxide if the vent is wrong. The vent material, slope, clearances and termination point all matter. This work should be done by a licensed plumber who follows the manufacturer's instructions and the state code that applies to your home.

Gas sizing is just as important. A tankless burner can demand much more gas than a tank heater. If the line is too small, the unit shuts down or runs poorly when other gas appliances run together. Your plumber should calculate the load for every gas appliance on that line before choosing the unit.

If you ever smell gas, leave the building. Call the gas utility or 911 from outside, and do not flip switches or use a phone indoors. Never try to fix a gas leak yourself. Georgia homes served by Atlanta Gas Light can use the utility's 24-hour emergency line from a safe distance.

Why Your Existing Plumbing Affects the Quote

Tankless units move a lot of water through narrow passages. They work best with clean supply lines, working shutoff valves and correct pressure. A plumber looks at the old connections, the relief valve discharge path and the drain nearby.

Older galvanized or heavily scaled fittings may crumble when disturbed. Replacing them at the heater adds parts and time. Many installers also add isolation valves so the unit can be flushed later. Those valves are inexpensive compared with the unit but they are labor to fit.

The home's pressure matters too. In Gwinnett County the pressure reducing valve on the house side of the meter is the homeowner's responsibility. A failing valve can send high pressure into a new heater, so a plumber often tests it during the visit. You can check static pressure at an outdoor spigot with an inexpensive gauge.

Extras That Add to the Bill

Several items appear on quotes that surprise homeowners. A condensate drain line is needed for high-efficiency gas units because they produce acidic water. Some need a neutralizer cartridge. A recirculation pump or return line shortens the wait for hot water but adds piping and a power source.

Other common extras include a new gas shutoff, a sediment filter, a dedicated electrical outlet for the control board and wall bracket reinforcement. Disposal of the old tank is usually a line item too.

Ask which of these are included and which are optional. A low base price that leaves out venting or a gas upgrade is not a lower price. It is a different scope. Compare quotes by listing the same items side by side.

How to Compare Quotes Fairly

Ask every installer for the same written scope. It should name the unit capacity, the fuel, the vent route, the gas line work, the electrical work, the valves and the permit handling if any applies. Permit rules vary, so ask the Peachtree Corners Building Division what applies to your address.

  1. Ask for the load calculation. A good quote shows why a unit size was chosen.
  2. Ask who handles the gas line. Make sure sizing is part of the plan.
  3. Confirm the vent material and route. It should match the unit instructions.
  4. Ask about the old heater. Confirm that removal is included.
  5. Check the license. Georgia publishes a free GOALS licensee search through the Secretary of State.

When a New Tank Is the Smarter Choice

Tankless is not right for every house. If the venting path is long, the gas line is far away or the panel cannot support an electric unit, the total cost climbs fast. A new tank heater may meet your needs for much less work.

Households with modest hot water demand can also do well with a tank. Large households with several bathrooms often gain more from a tankless system. We are happy to talk through both choices on our water heater replacement page before you decide.

Lifespan and savings claims vary by model, water quality and usage. Treat any promise of a specific payback period with caution until you see the numbers for your own home.
